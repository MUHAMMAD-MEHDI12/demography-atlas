About this atlas

Demography Atlas shows how the population of every country and area changes between 1950 and 2100. For each place, three age charts sit around it on the map: how the population is spread across ages, at which ages people die, and how old mothers are when children are born.

Grab the triangle and drop it on any country, click a country, or pick one from the list. Slide the years under the chart or press play, and use Compare to overlay a second place. Years up to 2023 are UN estimates; later years are UN projections.

About the lab

Data sources and how to verify them

Every population number on this atlas comes from the United Nations World Population Prospects 2024, published by the Population Division of the UN Department of Economic and Social Affairs. GDP per capita comes from the World Bank. Below is a comprehensive directory of every major population and demography data source on the internet.

UN figures can differ from those published by national statistics offices. The UN reviews each country's censuses, surveys and registration data, adjusts for gaps such as under-counting, and applies the same methods everywhere so that places and years can be compared fairly. When you check a number, compare it with the UN Data Portal rather than a national source.

Checks run when the data is built

  • Population on 1 July 2023 matches UN published figures exactly: World 8,091,735,000; India 1,438,070,000; China 1,422,585,000; Pakistan 247,504,000.
  • For every place and year from 1950 to 2100, births − deaths + net migration agrees with the UN population change to within 8,000 people; the small gaps come from rounding in the source tables.
  • Deaths by age, computed from UN death rates, add up to the official UN death totals within a median of 0.2%.
